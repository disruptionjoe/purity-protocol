# Exit-with-Public-History Closed-Baseline Tabletop

Status: working synthetic research; not an exit policy, rights posture,
contribution rule, gate, role, or eligibility decision.

## Question

Can the closed founder-only baseline keep a hypothetical exit, withdrawal, or
fork from being misrepresented as erasing public history, retained copies,
attributable creation, or downstream reliance?

## Construction and limits

This finite paper tabletop uses fictional records only. It supplies no person,
account, credential, contribution, license selection, legal conclusion, or
actual exit process. It does not establish which rights, duties, remedies, or
portable creations a future relationship could have.

| Case | Synthetic condition | Required closed-baseline result | Why erasure is refused |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1a | A hypothetical access revocation happens after a public artifact and a copied credential-shaped reference are recorded. | Stop future access; preserve a containment record and distinguish revocable access from retained knowledge, public artifacts, and any copied material. | Removing access cannot remove information already seen or public history. |
| E2a | A hypothetical author withdraws after a draft has been merged and cited by a downstream fictional record. | Stop new project-local reliance; preserve the withdrawal, the prior history, and a correction or disputed-status route. | A withdrawal request cannot erase citations, copies, reliance, or reputational inference. |
| E3a | A hypothetical exit request is conditioned on surrendering undisclosed attribution, retained-copy, or fork treatment. | Pause the hypothetical relationship; preserve the disputed terms and work boundary; refuse a portability or rights conclusion. | Departure pressure cannot resolve rights that were never made explicit. |
| E3b | A fictional fork retains a public history but cannot separate project and hypothetical outside creation. | Preserve the inseparable boundary and route any future treatment to a separately governed correction or fork decision. | A repository fork records history; it does not identify portable creation or repair attribution. |

## Result

The construction supports one narrow negative control: prospective stopping,
access removal, withdrawal labeling, and a repository fork may contain future
project-controlled activity, but none establishes erasure or full reversal of
knowledge, public history, reliance, rights already granted, or inseparable
creation. A future gate must retain a visible consequence record rather than
call an exit complete merely because current access ended.

## Disposition

Analytical advance only. Rights, provenance, attribution, documentation and
specification licensing, patent posture, legal readiness, and actual consent
remain `Unknown`. No exit terms, retention rule, role, gate, acceptance,
eligibility, or external action is created.
