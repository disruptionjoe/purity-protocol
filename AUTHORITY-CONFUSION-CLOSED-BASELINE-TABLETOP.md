# Authority-Confusion Closed-Baseline Tabletop

Status: **executed synthetic baseline - not a gate, role, policy, invitation,
decision, or evidence of real-world comprehension**

Authority: this Lane 1 artifact tests how Purity Protocol's current
founder-only authority resolves common inferences from its actual public
surfaces. It does not change those surfaces, make anyone eligible, test a
future role candidate, satisfy an evidence bundle, or bind Joe.

## Research Question

Can the current repository resolve claims that public visibility, the MIT code
license, merged analytical work, a fork, a relationship, or automation creates
authoritative effort eligibility, decision voice, or meta-governance voice
without relying on unwritten founder intent?

This is the negative-control question for the current **remain closed**
baseline. Architecture of Legitimacy owns the commissioned comparison and
testing of future role candidates. This tabletop does not preempt that work.

## Construction And Resolution Method

Each case starts with a plausible claim about what one public surface permits.
The claim is then traced through the repository's ratified authority order:

```text
CONSTITUTION.md
→ ratified decisions and amendments in DECISIONS.md
→ GOVERNANCE.md
→ current status and phase records
→ ROADMAP.md
→ README.md
→ AGENTS.md
```

A case:

- **passes** when current authoritative text yields one result, lower layers
  conform, and no unwritten intent is needed;
- **fails** when a current public surface actually grants or implies the
  claimed authority contrary to the hierarchy; and
- is **ambiguous** when two effective authority paths yield incompatible
  results or the result requires an unstated distinction.

Passing establishes only repository-internal resolvability. It does not prove
that a reader noticed, understood, accepted, or would comply with the result.

## Result

All eleven cases resolved without competing effective authority paths:

- ten denial cases correctly rejected authority or eligibility by implication;
- one positive control identified the formal constitutional change path while
  confirming that the path has not been used; and
- no case required private `pure-os` material, CapacityOS memory, or unwritten
  founder intent.

Case B2 carries a **scope warning**. The MIT text grants broad permissions in
the covered software, while the ratified license decision and public
orientation narrow what the project claims that decision settles. The
repository resolves the authority question, but documentation, specification,
patent, provenance, and attributable-creation rights remain **Unknown**. This
warning is evidence against treating the closed baseline as gate-ready.

## Negative Evidence And Falsifiers

The tabletop would have failed if:

- public visibility, a fork, a relationship, or automation produced a valid
  authority path not separately granted by the Constitution;
- a lower layer contradicted the Joe-only eligibility rule;
- merged research could acquire decision status without the recorded decision
  method;
- an ordinary decision could amend constitutional eligibility silently;
- the Architecture commission transferred Purity's gate authority; or
- the only way to resolve a case was private context or unwritten intent.

None occurred in the tested source state. The B2 rights warning remains
load-bearing rather than being treated as a pass on legal readiness.

## What This Baseline Does Not Establish

The result does not establish:

- that a real reader will find or understand the hierarchy;
- informed consent, contributor qualification, or a legitimate review and
  appeal path;
- documentation, specification, patent, provenance, or joint-creation rights;
- review or correction capacity;
- resistance to informal influence, collusion, capture, or social pressure;
- real-world exit, correction reach, or reversibility;
- that a future bounded-effort candidate will preserve the same separation; or
- gate readiness, satisfaction, adoption, activation, or Phase 0 graduation.

These limits prevent a synthetic governance-resolution pass from becoming a
claim about social or institutional behavior.

## Disposition And Comparison Rule

Current disposition: **closed-baseline synthetic support; future gate remains
not decision-ready**.

The current authority hierarchy is internally resolvable for the tested
confusion claims. A future candidate may be compared with this baseline only
after its role, rights, acceptance semantics, and attack conditions are
predeclared. The candidate should fail if it introduces an authority path that
cannot be distinguished from decision or meta-governance voice, depends on
unresolved rights, or makes stopping look like reversal.

Architecture of Legitimacy's commissioned engagement remains the research
owner for candidate comparison and testing. Purity Protocol retains the
recipient decision, gate, satisfaction, adoption, and activation authority.
